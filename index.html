Learn Once, Adapt Anytime: Unifying Explicit Segmentation and Pathwise Conditioning for Long-Horizon Manipulation

Author 1, Author 2, Author 3, Author 4,
Research Lab Group
Paper Code

Abstract

To effectively execute long-horizon manipulation tasks in dynamic environments, robots must possess the capability to rapidly adapt to novel and changing situations. Techniques such as Learning from Demonstration (LfD) enable intuitive skill acquisition without the need for explicit coding. However, scaling these methods to extended, multi-step tasks exposes critical limitations, as compounding errors constrain their ability to generalize to unforeseen Out-of-Distribution (OoD) scenarios. In this work, we present a probabilistic framework that tackles this challenge by combining a kinematically-driven task decomposition approach with movement primitive learning based on Gaussian Processes (GPs). To ensure robust execution across the entire task horizon, the algorithm incorporates a zero-shot adaptation mechanism to new via-points without requiring retraining, through Pathwise Conditioning. This approach is also capable of dynamically sequencing and adapting each subtask segmented on the fly, preserving continuity and irregular executions between subtasks.

General Overview of the Method

Proposed Framework is a probabilistic architecture designed for the robust execution of long-horizon manipulation tasks in dynamic environments.

To tackle the critical limitations of compounding errors and Out-of-Distribution (OoD) scenarios, our method combines a kinematically-driven task decomposition approach with movement primitive learning based on Gaussian Processes (GPs). The workflow operates in three primary phases:

1. Task Segmentation and Movement Libraries Generation

  • Kinematic Heuristic Segmentation: Complex demonstrations are autonomously decomposed into discrete subtasks by evaluating abrupt changes in acceleration and jerk (smoothness) against a dynamic threshold $\theta$.
  • Probabilistic Fusion: To counteract redundant fragmentation, initial heuristic cut points initialize a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM), iteratively merging contiguous points based on highly overlapping probability distributions.
  • Energy-Based Clustering: Segments are grouped into reusable skill libraries based on their spatial similarity, which is measured by the Gaussian Optimal Transport (GOT) energy cost required to transform one encoded GP into another.

2. Learning of Subtasks via Gaussian Processes (GPs)

  • Heteroscedastic Kernel Generation: Encapsulates each primitive using a GP that modulates a base squared-exponential kernel with local variance estimates, efficiently modeling the human execution's spatial dispersion.
  • Direct Via-Point Embedding: Constraints like exact routing through targets are directly embedded into the training framework. By constructing an augmented space of subtask data $D_i$ and noiseless via-points $\bar{D}_i$, the GP guarantees the generation of robust trajectories that perfectly intersect the specified constraints.

3. Subtask Adaptation via Pathwise Conditioning

  • Zero-Shot Adaptation: Real-time Pathwise Conditioning adapts the learned GPs to new spatial constraints or OoD object configurations without retraining or requiring additional demonstrations.
  • Matheron's Update Rule: By shifting from sampling after conditioning to applying a deterministic update directly onto a prior trajectory, the algorithm bypasses heavy computational bottlenecks. If the environment dictates modified via-points $\tilde{q}_i$ at times $\tilde{t}$, the updated trajectory $h_i^{post}$ is constructed instantaneously:

    $$h_i^{post}(t; \omega)=h_i^{prior}(t; \omega)+K_i(t,\tilde{t})K'_i(\tilde{t},\tilde{t})^{-1}(\tilde{q}_i-h_i^{prior}(\tilde{t}; \omega))$$
In short: The framework automatically segments continuous human demonstrations into reusable kinematic subtasks, encapsulates them as Gaussian Processes, and utilizes Pathwise Conditioning to instantly adapt trajectories to Out-of-Distribution environments while maintaining continuous subtask connectivity.

Real World Long-Horizon Manipulation Tasks

To validate the proposed method, we conducted 4 real-world experiments using a mobile manipulator robot. For each task, a single long-horizon demonstration was used to decompose and learn the respective motion libraries. QR-based visual detectors tracked object poses in real-time, allowing the system to seamlessly adapt to static Out-of-Distribution (OoD) setups and dynamic environmental changes on the fly. The evaluation scenarios are:

1) Preparing a Capsule Coffee

A complex sequential manipulation task requiring the robot to interact with a coffee maker, capsule, and mug. This task highlights the framework's ability to chain multiple precise subtasks together and seamlessly adapt to spatial variations in the object placements.

2) Sorting Objects into Boxes

A scenario focused on sorting small and large objects into designated boxes. It demonstrates the system's capacity to handle multi-stage pick-and-place operations and successfully adapt to dynamic shifts in target container locations without requiring retraining.

3) Rearranging Cans on a Shelf

An organizational task where the manipulator must move items to distinct levels of a shelf. This evaluates the robot's zero-shot spatial adaptability when handling constrained environments and adjusting interconnected via-points across a wide workspace.

4) Wiping a Table

A continuous, non pick-and-place manipulation task. This demonstrates the framework's versatility, proving that Pathwise Conditioning can preserve the shape, intent, and fluidity of human-demonstrated sweeping motions even when the target workspace constraints are altered.

Simulated Long Horizon Tasks

We evaluate long-horizon performance using 2 PyBullet-based benchmarks. The objective is to demonstrate the adaptive performance of the algorithm across different tasks in long-horizon environments. To this end, various tasks were designed where environmental elements are modified or the subtasks are reordered, allowing the robot to demonstrate its efficiency in solving these tasks without needing to generate a new policy. This is achieved by applying Pathwise Conditioning to the movement primitives learned with Gaussian Processes.

Franka Kitchen Environment

Evaluates long-horizon sequential skills and multi-step manipulation in a simulated kitchen domain. The Franka Kitchen environment consists of resequenceable culinary chores, for which we generated 25 distinct long-horizon tasks with 25 demonstrations each.

Table Environment

The Franka LHT environment features 3 tabletop distinct task: Domestic Assistive, Medical Waste Sorting, and Food and Beverage Packing, evaluated across multiple test variations.
